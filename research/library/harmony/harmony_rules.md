# Core Harmony Rules

## 1. Dorian Mode
**Characteristics:**
*   **Scale:** Minor scale with a major 6th degree.
*   **Formula:** 1 2 b3 4 5 6 b7
*   **Sound:** "Bright minor." Less melancholic than natural minor due to the major 6th, but darker than major.
*   **Usage:** Common in jazz, pop, and rock. Often used for a "cool" or "mysterious" vibe.

**Application:**
*   Use when you want a minor tonality but need to avoid the "sad" connotation of the natural minor.
*   The major 6th creates a distinctive sound when resolving to the tonic.

## 2. ii-V-I Progression
**Characteristics:**
*   **Core:** The most fundamental progression in jazz and pop.
*   **Chords:** ii (minor 7) -> V (dominant 7) -> I (major 7).
*   **Voice Leading:** Smooth movement of chord tones (e.g., 7th of ii moves to 3rd of V, 3rd of V moves to 1st of I).

**Variations:**
*   **Minor ii-V-i:** ii (minor 7) -> V (dominant 7) -> i (minor 7).
*   **Secondary Dominants:** Using V/V to lead to V.
*   **Substitution:** Replacing chords with related ones (e.g., iii for I, vi for ii).

**Application:**
*   Use for strong, resolved harmonic movement.
*   Ideal for jazz-influenced pop and electronic tracks.

## 3. Modal Interchange
**Characteristics:**
*   **Definition:** Borrowing chords from the parallel mode (e.g., borrowing from C minor while in C major).
*   **Common Borrowed Chords:**
    *   **iv:** Minor subdominant (adds a "sad" or "nostalgic" feel).
    *   **bVI:** Major chord built on the flattened 6th (adds a "heroic" or "epic" feel).
    *   **bVII:** Major chord built on the flattened 7th (adds a "rock" or "driving" feel).

**Emotional Effects:**
*   **iv:** Melancholy, introspection.
*   **bVI:** Brightness, surprise.
*   **bVII:** Energy, forward motion.

**Application:**
*   Use to add color and variety to standard progressions.
*   Effective for creating "lift" in choruses or "breakdown" moments.
