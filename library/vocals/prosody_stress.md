# Vocal Prosody & Phonetic Stress Rules

## 1. Prosody in Singing
**Definition:** The alignment of musical elements (pitch, rhythm, dynamics) with the natural speech patterns of the lyrics.

### Key Components:
*   **Intonation:** The melody of the voice. In singing, intonation is more pronounced than in speech.
*   **Rhythm:** The timing of syllables. Syllables on strong beats should be stressed.
*   **Stress:** Emphasis on certain syllables. Stressed syllables are often longer and higher in pitch.

### Prosody Rules for AI Synthesis:
1.  **Word Stress:**
    *   **Nouns/Verbs:** Usually stressed on the first syllable (e.g., **MUS**-ic, **DANCE**-ing).
    *   **Prepositions/Articles:** Unstressed (e.g., "in", "the").
    *   **AI Application:** Increase volume and pitch slightly on stressed syllables.
2.  **Sentence Stress:**
    *   **Content Words:** Nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs are stressed.
    *   **Function Words:** Prepositions, conjunctions, articles are unstressed.
    *   **AI Application:** Reduce duration of unstressed syllables to create a natural "flow."
3.  **Emotional Prosody:**
    *   **Happy:** Higher pitch, faster tempo, brighter timbre.
    *   **Sad:** Lower pitch, slower tempo, darker timbre.
    *   **Angry:** Stronger attack, wider pitch range, louder dynamics.

## 2. Phonetic Stress Patterns
### English Stress Patterns:
*   **Trochaic:** Stressed-Unstressed (e.g., **HAP**-py). Common in pop music.
*   **Iambic:** Unstressed-Stressed (e.g., a-**BOVE**). Common in ballads.
*   **Dactylic:** Stressed-Unstressed-Unstressed (e.g., **MEL**-o-dy).

### Vowel Modification:
*   **High Notes:** Vowels tend to open up (e.g., "ee" becomes "ih", "ay" becomes "ah") to facilitate resonance.
*   **AI Application:** Adjust formant frequencies for high notes to prevent a "thin" sound.

### Consonant Articulation:
*   **Plosives (P, B, T, D, K, G):** Should be crisp and clear.
*   **Fricatives (F, V, S, Z, Sh, Zh):** Should be smooth and not too harsh.
*   **AI Application:** Reduce plosive intensity for close-mic singing styles.

## 3. Lyric Pacing
*   **Fast Pacing:** Many syllables per beat. Common in rap and upbeat pop.
*   **Slow Pacing:** Few syllables per beat. Common in ballads and opera.
*   **Syncopation:** Placing syllables on off-beats to create rhythmic interest.

## 4. Global Vocal Prosody
*   **Indian Classical:**
    *   **Gamaka:** Ornamentation (glides, shakes) that adds expressiveness.
    *   **Svara:** Notes are not fixed; they can be bent.
*   **Flamenco:**
    *   **Melisma:** Many notes sung to one syllable.
    *   **Grit:** A rough, emotional quality in the voice.
*   **Chant:**
    *   **Monophonic:** Single melodic line.
    *   **Drone:** Constant background note.
    *   **Speech-like:** Rhythm is free and follows the text.
