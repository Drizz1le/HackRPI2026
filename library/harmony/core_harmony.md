# Core Harmony Rules

## 1. The Dorian Mode
**Definition:** A minor mode characterized by a **raised sixth scale degree** compared to the natural minor (Aeolian) scale.
**Formula:** 1 - 2 - b3 - 4 - 5 - 6 - b7
**Characteristics:**
*   **Mood:** "Happy-sad," mysterious, or bluesy. It lacks the tragic quality of the natural minor due to the major 6th.
*   **Key Identifier:** The presence of the **Major IV chord** (containing the raised 6th) is the strongest indicator of Dorian mode.
*   **Pedal Note:** Often sustained over a tonic drone.

**Dorian Chord Chart (Key of D):**
*   **i:** Dm7 (D-F-A-C)
*   **ii:** Em7 (E-G-B-D)
*   **III:** Fmaj7 (F-A-C-E)
*   **IV:** G (G-B-D) -> *Characteristic chord*
*   **v:** Am7 (A-C-E-G)
*   **VI:** Bb (Bb-D-F)
*   **vii°:** C#dim (C#-E-G)

**Common Progressions:**
*   **i - IV:** The "Dark Rock" vamp (e.g., Pink Floyd).
*   **i - ii - III - IV:** A bright, ascending minor progression.
*   **i - VII - VI - VII:** A modal cadence often used in film scores.

## 2. The ii-V-I Progression
**Definition:** The fundamental harmonic movement in Jazz and functional harmony, moving from Subdominant (ii) to Dominant (V) to Tonic (I).

### A. Major ii-V-I
*   **Chords:** ii7 - V7 - Imaj7
*   **Example (C Major):** Dm7 - G7 - Cmaj7
*   **Function:** Establishes the major key clearly. The V7 chord contains the leading tone (B) which resolves to the tonic (C).

### B. Minor ii-V-i (Dorian Context)
*   **Chords:** iim7(b5) - V7 - im7
*   **Example (C Minor):** Dm7(b5) - G7 - Cm7
*   **Function:** Creates a darker, more resolved minor feel. The V7 chord is often altered (b9, #9) to increase tension.

### C. Jazz Harmony Rules
*   **Voice Leading:** Chord tones should move by step or small intervals. The 7th of the ii chord often becomes the 3rd of the V chord.
*   **Tension/Release:** The 7th of the V chord (4th scale degree) resolves down to the 3rd of the I chord.

## 3. Modal Interchange
**Definition:** Borrowing chords from parallel modes (modes with the same tonic) to add color and avoid the "predictable" diatonic sound.

### Common Borrowed Chords (from Parallel Minor to Major Key):
*   **bIII (Major):** Borrowed from Dorian/Harmonic Minor. Adds a bright, heroic lift.
*   **bVI (Major):** Borrowed from Mixolydian/Aeolian. Adds a "plagal" or "rock" feel.
*   **bVII (Major):** Borrowed from Mixolydian. Creates a "backdoor" dominant feel.
*   **iv (Minor):** Borrowed from Dorian/Aeolian. Creates a "sad" minor iv in a major key (e.g., C - F - **Fm** - C).

### Common Borrowed Chords (from Parallel Major to Minor Key):
*   **III+ (Augmented):** Borrowed from Lydian.
*   **IV (Major):** Borrowed from Dorian.
*   **VII (Major):** Borrowed from Mixolydian.

### Application Rules:
1.  **Avoid Avoid Notes:** When using Dorian, ensure the melody emphasizes the raised 6th to distinguish it from Aeolian.
2.  **Pivot Chords:** Use chords that are diatonic to both the original key and the borrowed mode to smooth the transition.
3.  **Duration:** Borrowed chords are often used for 1-2 bars before returning to the home key.
