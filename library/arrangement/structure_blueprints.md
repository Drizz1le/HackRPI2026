# Song Structural Blueprints

## 1. EDM (Electronic Dance Music) Structure
**Time Signature:** 4/4
**Tempo:** 120-130 BPM (House/Trance), 140+ BPM (Drum & Bass)

### Standard Blueprint
1.  **Intro (16 Bars):**
    *   Filtered drums (high-pass filter opening up).
    *   Riser FX (white noise sweep).
    *   Kick drum enters at bar 9.
2.  **Build-Up (16 Bars):**
    *   Snare rolls increase in density (1/4 -> 1/8 -> 1/16 notes).
    *   Pitch riser (siren effect).
    *   Energy peaks at the last bar.
3.  **Drop (32 Bars):**
    *   Full energy. Kick, Bass, and Lead Synth.
    *   Minimal melody, maximal rhythm.
    *   Often split into two 16-bar halves (A and B variations).
4.  **Breakdown (16 Bars):**
    *   Drums drop out or become sparse.
    *   Melodic theme returns (often the "hook").
    *   Atmospheric pads.
5.  **Build & Drop (Repeat):**
    *   Second build is often longer or more intense.
    *   Second drop may add new elements (arpeggios, harmonies).
6.  **Outro (16 Bars):**
    *   Filtered drums (low-pass filter closing).
    *   Fade out or loopable ending.

### Layering Strategy
*   **Stems:** Kick, Snare, Hi-hat, Bass, Lead, Pad, FX.
*   **Frequency Management:** Low-end (Kick/Bass) is mono; High-end (Synths/Pads) is stereo.

## 2. Pop Song Structure
**Time Signature:** 4/4
**Tempo:** 90-120 BPM

### Standard Blueprint (ABABCB)
1.  **Intro (4-8 Bars):**
    *   Hook melody or drum groove.
    *   Goal: Establish the "vibe" immediately.
2.  **Verse (16 Bars):**
    *   Lower energy, narrative lyrics.
    *   Bass and drums are present but sparse.
    *   Melody is rhythmic and speech-like.
3.  **Pre-Chorus (4-8 Bars):**
    *   Tension builder.
    *   Melody rises in pitch.
    *   Drums often switch to half-time or add snare rolls.
4.  **Chorus (16 Bars):**
    *   **The Hook.** Highest energy, simplest melody.
    *   Full instrumentation.
    *   Lyrics are repetitive and memorable.
5.  **Post-Chorus (4-8 Bars):**
    *   Instrumental hook or vocal chop.
    *   Maintains energy without new lyrical content.
6.  **Bridge (8-16 Bars):**
    *   Contrast in melody and harmony.
    *   Often shifts to the relative major or minor.
    *   Builds back up to the final chorus.
7.  **Final Chorus (16 Bars):**
    *   Maximum energy. Ad-libs, harmonies, and key change (optional).
8.  **Outro (4-8 Bars):**
    *   Fade out or a definitive "stinger" ending.

## 3. Symphonic Structure
**Time Signature:** Variable (often 4/4, 3/4, or 6/8)
**Tempo:** Variable (Adagio to Presto)

### Standard Blueprint
1.  **Exposition:**
    *   Introduction of primary themes (Theme A and Theme B).
    *   Orchestration is clear and distinct.
2.  **Development:**
    *   Themes are fragmented, modulated, and recombined.
    *   Harmonic instability.
    *   Dynamic swells and contrapuntal weaving.
3.  **Recapitulation:**
    *   Return of Theme A and B in the home key.
    *   Sense of resolution.
4.  **Coda:**
    *   Concluding section.
    *   Can be short (tag) or long (extended development).

### Orchestration Layers
*   **Strings:** Foundation (Violins, Violas, Cellos, Basses).
*   **Woodwinds:** Melodic color (Flutes, Oboes, Clarinets, Bassoons).
*   **Brass:** Power and harmony (Horns, Trumpets, Trombones, Tuba).
*   **Percussion:** Rhythm and impact (Timpani, Cymbals, Snare).

## 4. Global Song Structures
*   **Indian Classical:**
    *   **Alap:** Slow, unmetered introduction of the Raga.
    *   **Jor:** Rhythmic introduction without drums.
    *   **Gat:** Fixed rhythmic cycle (Tala) begins.
    *   **Improvisation:** Solo sections over the cycle.
*   **Blues (12-Bar):**
    *   **AAB Pattern:** Line 1 (statement), Line 2 (repetition), Line 3 (response).
    *   **I-IV-V Progression:** Three chords dominate.
*   **Oral Tradition (World Music):**
    *   **Non-Linear:** Structure is defined by the length of the performance rather than fixed sections.
    *   **Cyclical:** Repetition of a core groove with gradual addition of instruments.
