# Global Composition & Arrangement Knowledge Base

## 1. Western Classical Composition
*   **Motivic Development:** Small musical ideas (motifs) are repeated, varied, and expanded throughout the piece.
*   **Form:** Sonata, Rondo, Theme and Variations.
*   **Orchestration:** Treating the orchestra as a palette of colors. Instruments are swapped to change timbre while maintaining the melody.

## 2. East Asian Composition (Chinese/Japanese)
*   **Melody-Centric:** Harmony is secondary to the melodic line.
*   **Pentatonic Foundation:** Most traditional music is based on the pentatonic scale.
*   **Space (Ma):** Silence is an active part of the music. Notes are not played continuously; the gaps between them are significant.
*   **Imitation:** Instruments often imitate natural sounds (birds, wind, water).

## 3. West African Composition
*   **Polyrhythm:** Multiple conflicting rhythms played simultaneously (e.g., 3 against 2).
*   **Call and Response:** A leader sings/plays a phrase, and the group answers.
*   **Drone and Ostinato:** A repeating rhythmic pattern (ostinato) provides the foundation for improvisation.
*   **Gyil Scale:** A specific 7-note scale used in West African harps and xylophones, often with unique tuning.

## 4. Middle Eastern Composition
*   **Maqam System:** Melodic modes that dictate not just the notes, but the emotional journey of the piece.
*   **Improvisation (Taqsim):** Solo sections that explore the nuances of the mode, often using microtones.
*   **Rhythmic Cycles (Iqaat):** Complex rhythmic patterns (e.g., 10/8, 9/8) that drive the music forward.

## 5. Latin American Composition
*   **Syncopation:** Accents on the off-beats (e.g., Salsa, Bossa Nova).
*   **Clave:** A 5-note rhythmic pattern that serves as the backbone of the music.
*   **Harmonic Richness:** Use of extended chords (7ths, 9ths, 13ths) and modal interchange.

## 6. Encyclopedic Composition Rules
*   **Unity and Variety:** A piece should have enough repetition to be recognizable, but enough variation to remain interesting.
*   **Tension and Release:** Music moves from instability (tension) to stability (release). This is achieved through harmony, rhythm, and dynamics.
*   **Dynamics:** The volume of the music changes to create emotional impact. Crescendo (getting louder) builds tension; Decrescendo (getting softer) releases it.
*   **Timbre:** The "color" of the sound. Changing instruments or playing techniques (e.g., pizzicato vs. arco) adds variety.
